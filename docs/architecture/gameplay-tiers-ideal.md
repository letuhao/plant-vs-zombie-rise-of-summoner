# Gameplay tier survey — the ideal

**Status:** idea phase, 2026-09-12. Not a spec. No build authorized.

**Program:** `gameplay-tiers` (survey only — it owns no build). Map (if one is ever needed) → `docs/architecture/gameplay-tiers-map.md`. This doc answers the owner's audit question — *"is there anything in gameplay mechanisms that still needs a tier, and what is the most missing piece?"* — reserves the `legion-unit` program name the repo already points at, and stops there. Rankings, curves, and costs belong to later specs, never here.

## Which loop this extends

- **Spine B — creature summon and fusion**: souls → pulls, fusion of duplicates into stronger forms, wild joins and capture as sibling intakes. A species tier tree would live here; the survey finds the existing ladders already cover it (see below).
- **Spine A — level up and power**: Dave's level is the main line, endless and uncapped; specimens, species, and types level from work they did. Any legion/stack tier must read this ladder, never a private one.
- **Spine C — item collection and progression**: the one place tiers already fully exist (ten-rung rarity ladder, five affix tiers, gem grades, set bonus tiers, per-role socket ceilings, action rungs) — the precedent every proposal below is measured against.
- **Places 3/4/5 — farm/hunt/defend, world-map adventure, world-stage empire**: legions march, garrisons hold, recruitment pulses arrive weekly. The missing legion-unit tier tree lives here.
- **Place 6 — Delve**: the live consumer of species threat (`Encounter` reads `thetaOffset` today).

The lawn stays the first core loop, not the whole game; expeditions stay forever and stay auto-resolve; no fourth stock, no class, no stamina gate, no new named loop — adding one is an owner edit to `the-loops.md`.

## What this is

In the player's language: an audit of every "tier" the game already has and the two it is asked about. Items already climb five affix tiers on a ten-rung rarity ladder; creature species already carry two ten-rung ladders (how dangerous, how special) plus per-specimen stars; legion troop stacks have *no* tier at all today — no experience, no upgrade path, no identity of their own — and the design study for them already exists, investigated and deferred, waiting for the owner to call it back. The single most missing piece is the legion stack's own upgrade container and experience, not a demon species tree — because species already have two ladders and stacks have zero.

In system language: a three-bucket inventory over demons/creatures, legions/general-creatures, and the item precedent; genre prior art with numbers; a shape recommendation (reserve `legion-unit`, do not invent a species evolution ladder); and the owner decisions that actually unblock work.

Load-bearing principles, restated where they bite (a downstream session reads this doc, not its links):

- **Every RPG feature lives in the RPG layer, never by changing what PvZ is.** A species tier, a stack upgrade, a legion unit program — all are RPG-layer data modeling in `FusionRpg.Core`/`FusionRpg.Data`. Nothing here proposes touching PvZ's board, spawn, or AI. "Does the lawn support stack tiers" is the wrong question; "does the RPG layer have the record/runtime for it, and is it wired?" is the right one.
- **One power ladder, no private `f(level)`.** Contests read `Θ` (linear, difference-based); magnitudes read `P(Θ)`. Its §10 inventory is closed — a power-shaped number not in it has no permission to exist. Two live violations are named below (`PlaceholderBattleResolver.Strength`, `BaseAtk/BaseDefense(member.Level)`) precisely so no third is added.
- **The balance surface is data.** Tier counts, thresholds, upgrade costs, respec prices, recruit rates, and unlock gates live in versioned tuning JSON, never a `const`. Structural constants (circuit size, capacity ceilings, `PowerVector.One`) stay `const` with a comment saying why they are not tunable.
- **No hard progression ceilings.** Tier tops are content breadth (more families, more paths), never a hard stop on growth. Per-slot budgets are the exempt structural kind and must say so. Absolute bounds throw, never silently clamp.
- **Gameless-first is capability, not the pitch.** Tier progression must stay usable with the game closed after unlock; the injector enriches, never gates.
- **Seed → concrete → per-player.** Seedsmith emits seeds (enums, offline, committed, no magnitudes). The runtime rolls concrete per player, seeded. The model writes identity; deterministic code writes magnitude — enforced by schema audit, never by review.
- **A guardrail validates the contract and closed enums, never a population count or generated text.** Corpus sizes (species rows, combination counts, base-type counts) are readings that grow as content ships, never acceptance values.

## Prior art

Numbers and failure modes, not vibes. Unverified flags kept as unverified.

- **Heroes 3 — seven dwelling tiers × base/upgraded, and the upgrade building is the tier mechanic.** Castle line: Pikeman (Atk 4, Def 5, 1–3 dmg, 10 HP, Spd 4, 60g, growth 14) → Halberdier (6/5, 2–3, 10 HP, Spd 5, 75g); Archer (6/3, 2–3, 10 HP, Spd 4, 12 shots, 100g, growth 9) → Marksman (double shot, 24 shots, Spd 6, 150g); Cavalier (15/15, 15–25, 100 HP, Spd 7, 1000g, growth 2) → Champion (16/16, 20–25, Spd 9, 1200g; jousting +5%/hex, +35–45% achievable); Angel (20/20, 50 dmg, 200 HP, Spd 12, 3000g + 1 gem, growth 1) → Archangel (30/30, 250 HP, Spd 18, resurrection, 5000g + 3 gems) — `heroes.thelazy.net/index.php/List_of_creatures`, `heroesofmightandmagic.com/heroes3/creaturescastle.shtml`, GameFAQs creature guide. Fort/Citadel/Castle: +50%/+100% dwelling growth, 500–5000g + wood/ore gates (`.../buildingscommon1.shtml`). **Failure modes, documented by players:** top dwellings price out (Titan/Black Dragon "atrocious resources"; Gold Dragon "most expensive dwelling altogether" — GameFAQs lvl-7 ranking thread); numbers trump tiers (Necropolis skeletons "best unit in the game" via necromancy volume; Conflux cheap/fast linearity — same thread); Phoenix wins on speed + resurrection + Armageddon immunity rather than raw stats. Steal: per-town dwelling upgrade stamping only that town's future recruits (the exact §6 per-source container precedent); flat small deltas per tier (+2 Atk, +1 Spd, +15g) rather than multipliers; growth counts as the real rarity (14 → 1/week).
- **TFT — star levels are pure duplicates arithmetic, and cost-gated access does the tiering.** 3× 1★ → 2★, 3× 2★ (9 total) → 3★, instant auto-combine on board+bench (`tft.ninja/guides/game-mechanics/champions/star-levels`); unit cost 1–5g = rarity/power bands; player level gates board size *and* shop rarity odds (`en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teamfight_Tactics`). Failure mode to avoid: a duplicate-driven tier without a sink becomes a lottery queue — TFT answers with shared-shop scarcity (your copies deny mine). Our analogue: fusion already consumes duplicates; a stack-tier must never become "hold 9 copies to click up."
- **DQM — ranks F→X are fixed identity, not progression, and synthesis is the tier ladder.** Eight ranks F,E,D,C,B,A,S,X (plus G for weakest in Dark Prince); rank fixed per monster, "relatively minimal impact"; S/X almost always synthesis-only; Joker 2 same-species re-synthesis at 20/50 raises rank twice (chimaera, slime, skeleton…); `+` value thresholds (+25 → up to C, +50 → up to A); special formulas yield otherwise-unobtainable monsters (`dragon-quest.org Monster synthesis`, GameFAQs Joker synthesis guide). Closest shape to our fusion + rarity: fixed rank as matchmaking/scarcity signal, synthesis count as the climb. Failure mode to steal: rank inflation without stat meaning — DQM keeps rank nearly cosmetic *except* as a synthesis gate, which is exactly how our rarity already behaves (gates recipes, stars, prices — never magnitudes directly).

## The shape

**The real question is *which shape*, and the survey splits it in two: species needs no new tier (content question, default no); legion stacks need their container + experience (structure question, deferred not decided).**

**1. Species tier tree — recommend against; the two ladders plus star already tile the space.**
`threatBand` (danger → Θ offset → magnitudes) × `rarity` (scarcity → counts, stars, recipes, prices) × per-specimen star (investment) × species level (work done) leaves no unanswered "how strong / how special / how invested" question an evolution rank would answer. A cross-species promotion ladder would additionally contradict the settled "species catalog is read-only" architecture and the owner's own "species is the root" call. If the owner still wants visible species *ranks* for hunting fantasy, the DQM shape fits best: a fixed, near-cosmetic rank derived from existing fields (never a third magnitude input), used only as synthesis/recipe gating — **ordered by the owner 2026-09-12 (open question 1), so this is now active content scope, not a gap the code implies.** Default: no *evolution* tree, ever. Owner-confirmed principle (2026-09-12, binds all future tier work): each tier/rank axis owns its own closed vocabulary — unifying all ranks into one shared enum is lazy design, collapsing distinct axes (scarcity vs hunting fantasy) into one name that can never say anything the other doesn't.

**2. Legion-unit tiers — the most missing piece; reserve the program, un-defer in slices.**
The investigated §§5–9 design already holds the full shape (per-source upgrade container + `MemberId`, stack XP with WoG-average merges, 1-of-2 paths with respec-priced switching, near-death promotion needing `count`, Survivor Title). Recommendation, sequenced thinnest-first:
  1. **Reserve `legion-unit` as a program name now** (map stub + vocabulary pointer; no build) so `commander-surface-ideal.md:232`'s stub, the `tasks/creature-standalone-todo.md:197` pointer, and future siege/world specs have an address that is not "the creature program's deferred section." This doc is that reservation's reasoning trail.
  2. **Wire first, build later:** `DevelopmentLevel`→`FoundLegion` stamp (both ends built), `WhoKind.Type` first table row + first world/siege caller, `slots.legion.perDevelopmentLevel` turn-on, season yield/movement terms. Each is small, each is already specified nearby, none needs the container.
  3. **Container + `MemberId` + `count` + XP as one structural change** (they are jointly required — promotion needs `count`, merges need XP, management needs `MemberId`; landing one without the others strands it).
  4. **Paths + respec, then promotion — Title goes its own way** (adversarial review overturned the bundle: promotion is roster-intake economy — `count`, a level-up event, a tunable base chance, all stack-scoped and tuning-reversible; Title is achievement/identity infrastructure — a twelfth value in a closed eleven-kind enum plus a permanent binding plus a nonexistent world-event trigger, with WoW's purely-cosmetic titles as the genre pattern and stat-granting as a deliberate, power-creeping departure. One arc, two features; promotion stays the last tier slice, Title graduates to its own deferred achievement/identity decision: stat vs cosmetic + 12th `ContainerKind` + trigger sources).
- **Private `f(level)` cleanup rides along, never as its own program:** `PlaceholderBattleResolver.Strength` (`Hp × Level`) and siege `BaseAtk/BaseDefense(member.Level)` collapse into Θ/`P(Θ)` reads (threat-offset pattern already proven in `Encounter`) the moment stacks carry a real Θ source. No third formula is ever added.

Alternatives rejected, with reasons:
- **Mutable species catalog** (empire-upgradable species rows). Rejected: read-only everywhere; owner's root-template call; empire-wide bonuses already have carriers (`WhoKind.Type`, `CreatureType` scope) that don't mutate content.
- **Full `UniqueActor` per stack.** Rejected: blurs Axis 1 (general vs unique); army scale forbids it (dozens vs thousands); light `MemberId` + container is the sized alternative.
- **Third channel classification for tiers.** Rejected by the Stats-row warning: nine-class `UnitClass` + six render states already exist and are verified against consumers; a tier display rides catalogs, never a new taxonomy.
- **Duplicate-count star tiers for stacks (TFT shape).** Rejected: fusion already owns duplicate consumption for uniques; stacks are count-based headcounts, and a 9-copy click-up contradicts the troop-stack model.
- **Exact-count runeword-style host restriction for combinations as the legion pattern.** Different program; documented in `strain-splice-host-ideal.md` (this session) — `minSockets` stays a floor there for the same top-up-economy reason a stack floor must stay a floor here.
