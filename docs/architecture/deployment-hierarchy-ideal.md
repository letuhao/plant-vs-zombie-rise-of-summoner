## Which loop this extends

From `docs/guide/the-loops.md` / `docs/guide/the-game.md` (RPG + empire building; you are Crazy Dave; home holds the roster; lawn is the first core, not the whole war):

| Loop | How this serves it |
|---|---|
| **Spine B — Creature summon and fusion** | Unique demons are individuals with specimen progression; the roster/home is the root parent every deployment hangs off |
| **Place 1 — Lawn, first core** | Lawn-run Bound deploy is one child kind: a specimen leaves the parent with what it has, fights, comes back changed |
| **Place 4 — World map adventure + Place 5 — World stage empire** | Legions/sectors/garrisons are parent places; troop sources live here at army scale |
| **Place 6 — Dungeon crawler, the Delve** | Delve party slots are children with the strictest carry rules (pools, hunger, nerve, Downed, haul) |
| **Place 3 — Farming, hunting, defending the empire** | Siege is a child kind: garrison/legion members enter a board and return as casualties, not fresh troops |
| **Place 2 — Idle expeditions** | Retrieval missions: when the ground that holds a corpse-cache is gone (a sealed delve) or too hot, an expedition or bounty hunt goes back for the gear — timed, priced, and success-rolled like any other expedition |
| **Place 7 — Quests and events** | Failure branches: a death opens a retrieval quest (corpse-cache with a decay clock), not just a wipe screen |

It does not invent a new loop. It does not make the lawn the whole game. Same roster, same souls, same world, whichever place you play from.

## What this is

Today a unit always arrives **full**: every battle, delve room, lawn bind, and siege board starts its pools at rest-max and its status list empty, no matter what the unit just survived. This idea replaces that with a **deployment tree**:

- A **parent** is where a force lives: the home roster, a world-map legion in a sector, a siege garrison, an expedition muster.
- A **child** is a deployment of that force somewhere: a Bound lawn spawn, a delve party slot, a siege combatant, an expedition seat.
- When a child is created it **inherits** a snapshot of the parent's resources and sticky statuses. When the child settles (die, extract, board end, wipe, turn close) it **writes back** deltas to the parent: wounds, spent pools, gained nerve stacks, Downed markers — never a rewritten past.

Player sentence: *my tired, hungry, shaken crew deploys tired, hungry, and shaken — and if they get mauled, they come home mauled, not refreshed.*

**Lossiness (enrichment 2026-09-13, owner-locked):** death is not always a round trip. A unique demon that dies on a lawn run or on the world map counts as **injured** — graded in multiple tiers, not a binary flag. In a delve the same injury applies, except a **hardcore delve kills permanently**. A serious injury left without cure can itself turn permanently fatal. And a dead unique **drops its items where it fell**: another party can march out and pick them up — unless the ground is gone (a sealed delve), in which case expeditions and bounty hunts are the way back in. What is never recovered **decays** and may be lost forever. Troops are exempt from all of this: a general-demon legion is a bundle of units in the Heroes-of-Might-and-Magic stack sense — it carries no inventory, and its dead stay dead.

Load-bearing principles, restated inline because a downstream session reads this doc, not its links:

1. **Every RPG feature lives in the RPG layer; it is never built by changing what PvZ is.** Deployment inheritance is Cold/Data + Core bookkeeping (pool values, status specs, FSM phases) resolved through our own stack. It never needs a Unity field for "tired" and never reads PvZ's current state — the RPG observes past events and contributes signed deltas later.
2. **Two async systems; record-then-drain; deltas not absolutes.** A deploy snapshot is a Cold record; mid-fight HP moves as Funnel deltas (FA10 Add), never `mode=set`. Delay is the designed degradation.
3. **One ActorHub compose, one read.** Every child that needs combat derived/AppliedCombat contributes via `IActorStatSubsystem`/registered atom readers or consumes Hub output only. No per-deployment composer, no private fold. Dual compose is grandfathered debt until fused, never a template. Guard: `scripts/guard-actor-hub.ps1`.
4. **One power ladder.** Contests read `Θ` (linear difference); magnitudes read `P(Θ)`. Inheritance copies pool *values* and status *specs*; it never invents a new `f(level)`. `ssot-power-scale.md` §10 is closed.
5. **The balance surface is data.** Every rate, threshold, persist mask, and replenish number below lives in `data/tuning/`, never a `const`.
6. **No hard progression ceilings.** Recovery may be slow, gated, or priced — never a silent clamp. Absolute bounds throw.
7. **Gameless-first is capability.** The whole tree must work with Fusion closed. The injector enriches a fielded unique (live HP/status display); it never gates roster, legion, delve, or siege bookkeeping.
8. **Three stocks only (souls, essence, loam); six actor pools only (hp, stamina, hunger, spirit, qi, poise).** Loam is never a seventh actor pool — a battle may hold a side-scoped transient depot seeded from world stock, reconciled through the outcome record. A stock and a pool are different scopes; spending in battle is a sink, paying out loam from battle is forbidden (it would collapse the farming throttle).
9. **SQL only inside `FusionRpg.Data`.** All parent/child rows live behind `RpgStore*`; MatchRuntime stays RAM-only and SQL-free.

## The shape

### The tree

```text
parent (where a force LIVES)                child (where it DEPLOYS)
roster / home                                ├─ lawn Bound (1 specimen)
legion in sector (troops + attached uniques) ├─ lawn Bound (attached unique)
                                             ├─ siege combatant (troop slice + attached uniques)
                                             ├─ delve party slot (bound unique)
                                             └─ expedition seat (bound unique)
garrison (sector defense)                    └─ siege combatant (defense)
delve party (between rooms)                  └─ next room setup (same members, carried pools)
```

- **Uniques** are instance rows (`rpg_unique_actors`) with phase FSM. A child references `instanceId`; settlement writes back to that row. Never duplicated, never inferred from `typeId` (decisions.md Creature progression source row).
- **General troops** are troop-stack counts (`WorldEntityMember` count × species) with aggregate `Hp/Wounds`. They use HoMM3 top-unit semantics at resolve time; they never become N `UniqueActor` rows.
- **Attached uniques** (later): a legion member row may carry `InstanceId` (the column exists, `WorldState.cs:278`, all writers omit it today). Until then, attachment is roster→child directly; legion→child for uniques stays out.
- **Every combat read goes through ActorHub.** Children contribute (pools via `ActorResourcePools`, statuses via `StatusRuntime` + Hub `status.*` channels, gear/aptitude/tree via existing Hub paths) or consume Hub output. No child invents a composer.

### Inherit (parent → child)

A child setup carries three things, all **data**, all snapshotted at deploy time:

1. **Pool values** — the six current values (`ActorResourcePools.FromStored`), seeded through `CarryInPools`-shaped fields on every setup kind (battle already has the field, `BattleModels.cs:192`; delve/siege/lawn get the same shape). Missing entry = at-rest Max (today's behavior, kept as the explicit default).
2. **Status specs** — `BattleStatusSpec`-shaped declarative list (already the `DelveMemberState.Statuses` shape; nerve excluded — nerve is exclusively derived from stacks + spirit). Applied through `StatusRuntime.Apply` at the child's first tick with full resistance (never pre-applied silently).
3. **Sticky flags** — `Downed/DownedOnce`, `NerveStacks`, `Wounds`, phase (`Roster` required to deploy; `Recovering`/`ActiveBound` refuse).

What does **not** cross: live `StatusRuntime` instances (always re-applied at the child), Unity `ptr`s (rebound per child: lawn `UniqueBindings`, future siege/delve bindings), lawn sun bank (match-scoped `pvz.*`), souls/essence/loam stocks (player/world scope — a battle may receive a side-scoped transient **depot**, never an actor pool).

### Settle (child → parent)

Settlement is exactly-once per child, idempotent on `(parentId, childId)`:

| Child end | Writes back |
|---|---|
| Lawn die / board end | ActiveBound→Roster (existing one-write path) + specimen XP ledger; wounds as pool deltas where the parent tracks pools (delve members), else as `Wounds` on the legion row |
| Delve room clear | `CarryOut` pools + appended status specs + nerve stacks into `DelveMemberState`; hunger charged per room; downed recorded |
| Delve extract / wipe | Full settlement: `Recovering` rows with countdown **in delves** (existing), `downedOnce` sticky, permadeath-gated `Retired`, cross-delve persist subset (`AttritionPersistAcrossDelves`); **extract:** haul banked; **wipe (V1/V4, closed):** nothing banked or returned — the whole pack (corpse-gear + carry-in + haul) becomes one corpse-cache pinned to the room instead |
| Siege board end | Troop casualties decrement counts (with experience dilution per Total War); attached uniques settle like lawn deaths |
| Expedition return | Existing XP/materials path; fatigue/wound rows when this program adds them |

**Wave scope (clearing 2026-09-13):** wave 1 = lawn + delve + expedition retrieval. World-map and siege injury/cache/drop paths are **tracked dependencies** of the world-map and siege programs — this program specs the shared machinery (tiers, caches, decay, retrieval) once, and the world/siege edges land when those programs produce real deaths. No stub combat is specced to stand in.

Recovery clocks, in order of preference: **in delves** (delve-counted, existing `Recovering` precedent — no wall clock inside the game), then **world turns** (legion rest in owned ground with supply, Total War-gated: no replenish under attrition), then priced ritual (existing soul sink). Wall-clock recovery is rejected for game state (expeditions already own the wall clock; game state must replay byte-identically).

### Death, injury tiers, and who dies for real (enrichment 2026-09-13 — owner-locked)

Death on the lawn and on the world map does **not** retire a unique demon — it **injures** it, on a multi-tier ladder (counts, not a flag):

- **Tier shape (Battle Brothers / XCOM):** severity reads **%HP lost past a relative threshold** (harder to maim high-max-HP demons, easier to maim fragile ones). Each tier is a `wound.*`-family status container of atoms (exhaustion-shaped: timeless, attacker-less), so a wounded demon fights **progressively weaker** — no full-strength-at-1-HP. **Corrections (strengthen F5-power/F6-spiral):** (a) attacker-less `StatusRuntime.Apply` **excludes** the power contest by design (`ResistanceEvaluator.cs:294-302` — no real attacker side), so wound application is power-neutral; the `Θ`/potency reads happen at *injury grading time* (how bad is this hit, via the existing combat math), not at status-apply time — the doc's first draft blurred the two. (b) The anti-spiral guarantee is **not** runtime-owned: it is an opt-in constructor check per policy (`ExhaustionPolicy.cs:59-66`, mirrored `NervePolicy.cs:68-74`; nothing in `StatusCatalog.Register` or `StatusRuntime.Apply` enforces it), so the `wound.*` family ships **its own policy ctor with the same check**, or it ships unprotected. (c) The worsening counter lives in **durable per-specimen state** (same family as `rpg_unique_actor_pools` / `rpg_unique_actor_recovery`, `RpgStore.cs:547-554`; mid-delve it rides party state like `NerveStacks`) — `StatusInstance` carries no count, and the first draft named no home.
- **Pin parents (strengthen F10):** a cache hangs off a durable parent row with a stated lifetime per place — delve room (`rpg_delve_rooms`, header never deleted), map sector (`rpg_world_sectors`), expedition id (`rpg_expeditions`, members keyed `(expedition_id, instance_id)`). Lawn matches and siege boards have **no** durable parent row (lawn sessions are deleted on settle; siege setups are ephemeral), so those caches re-key to the **specimen's death receipt** (the `rpg_unique_lawn_xp_receipts` key shape: instance + match + occurrence) and live in the world/roster scope, not the match scope.
- **Delve:** the same ladder applies room to room, riding the existing `DelveMemberState` carry (`Pools`/`Statuses`/`NerveStacks`/`DownedOnce`). **Hardcore delve (permadeath rung): death is death** — the existing `PermadeathGate` + `Retired`-at-extraction rule stands unchanged, now joined by corpse-drop below.
- **Untreated wounds worsen (lossy):** a serious tier left without cure (rest, supplies, ritual, temple-equivalent) can advance a tier per settlement clock and eventually kill — `Retired` with the same producers' discipline (idempotent, named reason). This is the Darkest-Dungeon affliction pressure and the XCOM fatigue→injury conversion, formalized as a counter, never a wall clock.
- **Troops:** a legion stack uses HoMM3 top-unit math (`effectiveHp = Hp − Wounds` already in `BuildAnimateSetups`); casualties decrement the count with experience dilution. No tiers, no recovery rows, no inventory — the dead stay dead.

### Corpse-drop, decay, and retrieval (enrichment 2026-09-13 — owner-locked)

When a unique demon (or commander — same assignment table shape) dies where gear can fall:

1. **Drop:** when a unique demon (or commander) **really dies** — hardcore-delve `Retired`, or a wound tier that worsened all the way — its assigned **rolled** items **move from the assignment tables into a corpse-cache pinned to the place of death** (lawn match / map sector / siege board / expedition id), instead of today's rule. Downed-but-`Recovering` members do **not** drop: nothing died, the gear stays worn. Stock-grade kit never drops (counters have no instance row to decay; re-issue is free). **Overturn note:** `item/ssot-inventory.md:452` row *"Specimen dies in a match: Nothing … Gear is never lost on death"* is overturned for the real-death case, and `:453` *"Retire releases every assignment to the armoury"* is amended: on a **death**-Retire the release target is the **corpse-cache**, not the armoury (release-to-armoury stays for voluntary retire/fusion). **Correction (strengthen F2/I1):** the first draft said "assignment rows are not deleted — they point at the cache." Unbuildable: `rpg_item_assignment` is `PRIMARY KEY (specimen_id, role)` with no cache column (`RpgStore.Items.cs:152-159`), so repointing two dead specimens' same-role rows at one cache id would collide and `ON CONFLICT(specimen_id, role) DO UPDATE` would silently replace. The cache is therefore a **new table** (`rpg_corpse_cache` family in `FusionRpg.Data`), populated by **moving** rows out of both assignment tables (unique **and** commander pouch `rpg_player_item_assignment`, `RpgStore.Items.cs:164-171 — the first draft named only the first). Moved rows vanish from `ListAssignments`, so the deploy-time full-rebuild projection (`RpgStore.Items.cs:840-874`) cannot resurrect dropped gear — the exclusion rule falls out of the move, no filter needed.
2. **Decay (tunable, deterministic) — clock start and rate locked (V6, 2026-09-13):** every cache carries a decay clock, but the clock does not start at the moment of death — it starts when the **place's own session ends**, so a live party can still walk back and retrieve without racing a timer. For a delve room that is **`CloseDelve`** (extract or wipe) — the cache sits inert while the delve run is still open, exactly as today's floor items already do; for lawn/siege it is immediate, since those sessions have no durable holding row to begin with (V5, below). Once the clock starts it runs on the **world-stage turn** (`TurnCalendar.cs:13` — one turn is one day; the highest, and only non-wall, clock in the hierarchy), frozen while no one is present to advance it (idle players cause no decay). At each elapsed turn a per-item survival roll runs (EVE Loot-Fairy shape: independent per stack, not all-or-nothing); failures destroy the item row with a logged reason. Better caches decay slower (Rust shape: contents scale the window), but no cache is immortal. **Starting-shape target (V6): a full decay ≈ 112 turns** — one complete 4-season calendar cycle under the shipped `world.v5.json` calendar (`daysPerWeek 7 × weeksPerMonth 4 × 1 month/season × 4 seasons`), not a wall-clock year — a tunable, not a promise. **Correction (strengthen F1-decay):** every decay tick runs on a **named seeded sub-stream** (`SeededRng.DeriveStream` per-item derivation, expedition `tick:{t}` precedent) with an **idempotency key per (cache, tick)** — a replayed settlement re-derives identical rolls and never double-destroys. `System.Random`, wall clocks, and unkeyed ticks are rejected by the same no-clock guard shape as `spec-delve-attrition.md:387-389`.
3. **Field retrieval:** deploy another party to the cache's place and reach it — items transfer back to the armoury/assignee on success, claimed under an **idempotent claim key per (cache, party)** so a field party racing an expedition to the same cache has exactly one winner. Success is **rolled against retriever strength** (tunable: retriever `Θ` vs cache depth/threat — the contest reads `Θ` difference, the same regime as every other contest; see correction on `standing` below).
4. **Expedition / bounty retrieval:** when the place is gone or unreachable (a sealed/extracted delve, deep hostile ground), the cache surfaces as a **retrieval expedition** (timed tier, priced in souls like any expedition) or a **bounty hunt** — a player-posted request run by the player's own empire units as an expedition-kind mission, surfaced through a **bounty quest** (identity locked 2026-09-13; no NPC hunter faction). **Correction (strengthen E1):** this needs a **new expedition kind + a cache-target column** (`rpg_expeditions` today has no target, `spec-expeditions.md:46-47`) and a manifest shape that includes gear-return (`:38` manifest has no gear seat) — filed as an ask on the expedition program, not smuggled in. Tarkov-insurance shape: priced, partial, delayed — with our clocks instead of 24-hour timers. Unclaimed past the window = decayed = lost (Tarkov's unclaimed-returns rule).
5. **Scope lock:** lost inventory affects **unique demons and commanders only, rolled rows only**. Troop stacks carry no items and their deaths resolve as counts (HoMM3). Stock-grade kit (fungible counters) never drops — only **rolled rows** can be lost, which bounds both the Data shape and the player's grief. (Closed as decided — see Q7 removal below: stock counters have no instance row, so per-item decay cannot apply to them.)
6. **Anti-fraud (strengthen M1):** the drop set is **snapshotted at deploy time** (what the demon carried into the fatal deployment), not at death time — stripping a doomed demon naked mid-fight cannot dodge the lossy stake (the Tarkov insurance-fraud shape, closed in advance). Voluntary pre-deploy unequip in `Roster` stays free; the snapshot is what makes that choice happen *before* risk, not during it.
7. **Dead ends with named fates (strengthen M2–M4):** a cache whose place changes state gets a fate, never limbo — sector lost or siege board wiped: cache transfers to the victor's salvage or decays immediately (tunable, stated per place); sealed delve with no retrieval posted: cache **moves to the void** (V2, closed — the void is the one named sink, not limbo; a bounty is simply what a map-less mission looks like when it targets a void-resident cache, never a second competing auto-post fate); idle player: delve/turn clocks freeze (no Rust-style ambush while away — the WoW lesson is that time penalties just log players out; pressure comes from decay-on-play, not decay-while-away).

### Baggage, revisit loot, and the void (enrichment track, briefed 2026-09-13; V1/V3/V4/V5/V6 locked 2026-09-13)

- **Baggage is `loot-pack`, not a new system (V3, closed by reuse).** The container survivors loot a dead teammate's gear into is the already-approved `PackGrid` (`party-dungeon/spec-loot-pack.md`, approved 2026-09-05, unbuilt) — a 4×10 Diablo-2-style grid per party per delve, footprint-derived cells, first-fit auto-arrange, floor overflow for what does not fit. No new slot family, no new table. Picking up a corpse's rolled gear is a `pack.move`-shaped decision like any other haul pickup: rolled rows only, capacity-gated by the existing grid, certain-on-reach (Q3b, no second roll). The drop set is the **deploy-time snapshot**, so mid-fight stripping can't dodge the stake and pre-deploy unequip in `Roster` stays free. It can happen live, room to room, at any point the party is physically present — there is no decay pressure to rush it, because decay (below) does not start until the delve session ends.
- **Wipe overturns `loot-pack` §7 (V4, closed — overturn, not smuggled):** on **any** delve wipe — the permadeath rung (real death, already owner-locked) or a sub-permadeath wipe where every member only goes to `Recovering` — **nothing returns home automatically.** The whole party's pack (assigned corpse-gear + carry-in gear + unspent carry-in stock + haul) becomes one cache pinned to the delve room. This **overturns `spec-loot-pack.md` §7's shipped-as-approved "Wiped" rule** (*"the haul banks nothing — haul rows destroyed… carry-in gear and unconsumed carry-in stock return home"*): carry-in gear no longer returns home, and haul no longer destroys — both move into the cache instead. Owner's framing: a delve is a hardcore feature end to end, not a casual one — a cure can save the party, but retreat has a real cost, and that cost is walking out with nothing until someone (the same party on revisit, or a later retrieval mission) goes back for it. **Filed as an ask on the party-dungeon/`loot-pack` owning program** (below) — an approved spec does not change silently.
- **Revisit loot:** the cache stays pinned to its delve room and is lootable on revisit (by the same party mid-run, or a later party) for as long as the room has not closed out; claims are idempotent per (cache, party); **a failed retrieval never deletes the cache** (D2 multi-corpse lesson — wipes leave it, minus whatever decay has already taken). Revisit reads the place's own clock, never wall time.
- **The void — single home (V2, closed):** a cache with **no friendly presence left and no reachable place** moves to the void — a *named sink with rows*, not limbo (M2–M4's "no silent void" meant limbo; the Void is the addressed sink that answers it). There is exactly one home for an unreachable cache: the void. A "bounty" is not a second, competing fate — it is simply what a map-less retrieval mission looks like when it targets a void-resident cache (§4 below); nothing auto-posts a bounty as an alternative to the void.
- **Void entry timing (V5, closed):** lawn and siege caches enter the void **immediately** — there is no durable place-row to hold a grace window for a lawn match or a siege board (both are deleted or ephemeral on settle, per the Pin-parents finding above), so a grace window would mean inventing a new durable holding row this doc's own shape does not have. Delve caches, by contrast, sit at their room (not the void) until the delve session itself closes — see decay timing below.
- **Decay clock (V6, closed) — start and rate:** the decay clock does not start at the moment of death. For a **delve** cache it starts at **`CloseDelve`** (extract or wipe) — nothing decays while the run is still open and the party (or a teammate) could still walk back to the room. For **lawn/siege/world** caches the clock starts immediately, since those places have no "still open" state to wait out. Once running, decay ticks on the **world-stage turn** (`TurnCalendar.cs` — one turn is one day; the only non-wall clock above this program), frozen while no one advances it. **Starting-shape target: a full decay ≈ 112 turns** — one complete 4-season calendar cycle under the shipped `world.v5.json` calendar, not a wall-clock year (turns are player-paced End Turns, not real time) — a tunable, never a promise. **Void decay ticks in the `Events` phase of `TurnEngine.Step`** on named seeded sub-streams with per-(cache, tick) idempotency keys; per-item independent survival rolls (EVE shape), contents-scaled window (Rust shape), never immortal.
- **Map-less missions:** a new expedition kind (or sibling) whose target is a *cache id*: no squad-on-a-board, no interactive fight. Input = cache ref + souls price; output = gear-return manifest seat beside souls/materials/XP; timed on expedition wall-clock (the one legal wall clock). Requires the filed expedition ask (kind + target column + gear seat).
- **Grounding (brief inventory):** pack grid state + lock table + haul appender + replay-safe lock writes all built (`RpgStore.Delve.cs:17,126,1089,1120,1161,1190,1237`); `CarryInPools` never populated by the carry path, pack-lock refusal on salvage/assign owed, floor items unjoined (wiring gaps); void/revisit-loot/retrieval-kind/map-less shape, and the `loot-pack` §7 wipe-rule amendment, are all real gaps (zero `src/` hits for void-cache; `spec-loot-pack.md` ships the wipe rule this doc now overturns).

### Item durability, wear, and repair (enrichment track, briefed 2026-09-13; D1–D6 locked 2026-09-13)

- **Property + storage:** per-rolled-instance `(max, current)`, `long`, integer-exact. Max is **DERIVED** from already-authored inputs (`class` · rarity · `tags`, `item/seed-contract.md:84,231`) — never authored. Current is mutable head state beside `enhance_level`; decremented at settlement, restored by repair ops. **Zero = unusable until repaired, as the base state (D1, closed)** — no gradual stat decay (full power until 0) — but repair itself is not risk-free; see Repair below for the destruction path. Stock-backed assignments never wear (counters have no instance row — same split as drop, confirmed compatible, no fourth-stock shape). **Commander-pouch gear wears identically (D6, closed):** same `(max, current)` shape, same wear/repair mechanism, scoped to the commander-pouch assignment table instead of the unique-actor one — consistent with corpse-drop already treating commanders the same as uniques.
- **Battle wear (D3, closed — per-battle only):** settlement-time decrement, once per battle, deterministic, seeded, idempotent per (instance, battle) — never mid-fight, never wall-clock, and **never per delve room**: a long delve wears gear no harder than the same number of battles fought anywhere else — only battle count matters, not room count. Inputs: `roundsParticipated`, attack/hit tallies where available (`BattleModels.cs:511` already emits `HpRemaining/DamageDealt/Kills/Survived`), deployment kind. Shape precedent: `StructurePolicy.RepairCost` (`checked(cost × missing × ratioMilli / max / 1000)`, widen-first, divide-last). Wear is arithmetic on tallies — not a `Θ` contest, not a curve.
- **Death-drop extra decay:** one-shot chunk off current at move-to-cache time (tunable ‰ of max + flat floor), on the corpse-decay sub-stream with per-(cache, tick) idempotency. Stacks with battle wear; never touches max; replaces the "second roll at the cache" (Q3b certain-on-reach stands — decay *before* arrival is the gamble, arrival itself is certain).
- **Repair — two tiers, graded by damage and material on hand (D2, closed):** field touch-up and workbench repair are the same verb at different completeness, not two outcomes chosen up front. **Field touch-up** (D4, closed — ships in v1): substrate-only, at camp/rest or mid-expedition, caps out at a **partial/eroding** restore — it can only do as much as the material on hand and the damage allow. **The party must physically carry the repair tool and materials** (pack-grid cells / actor inventory — the same carry economy as `loot-pack` provisioning, no abstract camp stockpile); no tool or materials in the pack, no field repair available. **Workbench repair** (home) uses the full class vocabulary — **souls** (flat fee) + **substrate.{frame}.{grade}** + **catalyst.temper** — and normally restores to full, but insufficient material for the damage severity falls back to the same partial/eroding result as a field touch-up (Fire Emblem's "partial repair if short" precedent) rather than refusing outright. **Shard leg at high rungs (D5, closed):** at low/mid rungs repair stays substrate + temper + souls only; **top-rung (ceiling-content) repair additionally consumes a shard leg**, tying the most expensive repairs to the same scarce material that gates ceiling-tier crafting. **Destruction risk (D1, closed):** a repair attempt is not risk-free — every attempt carries a tunable failure chance that **destroys the item permanently** instead of restoring it, rather than a guaranteed-safe maintenance action; the safer field/partial tier and the riskier workbench/full tier may carry different failure rates (a balance question, not an architecture one). Recipe rows reuse `material_recipe` shape; repair needs a new priced verb + new `op_kind` — both namespaces closed at ten, so reviewed ask-first adds. Cost shape: `repairCost = soulsFee + forgeLeg × missingFraction × repairRatioMilli` (siege 600‰ precedent, bounded 0..1000). Repair never raises max; enhancement still owns `+n`.
- **Grounding (brief inventory):** durability named DERIVED with zero implementation (`seed-contract.md:84`, `defect-register.md:194`, `DelvePrices.cs:15`); assignment PKs, full-rebuild projection, disposition vocabulary, mutation ledger (I6, `op_kind` closed at ten), recipe/cost-class/salvage-converter machinery all built; durability column, wear consumer, price derivation, repair verb/`op_kind`/recipes, broken-at-zero semantics, and the repair-failure destruction path are all real gaps.

## Program-level acceptance (strengthen U6 — no module is done on its own criteria alone)

> **A real loss and a real recovery, end to end:** a geared unique dies a real death, its rolled gear lands in a cache that decays on a seeded clock; a second party retrieves part of it through a strength-rolled mission; an unreachable cache returns partial gear through a priced expedition; replaying every settlement step changes nothing; and a troop stack that dies leaves counts, never caches.

Broken-at-zero — CLOSED: unusable is the base state, but repair carries a destruction risk.** An item at zero durability is unusable until repaired (never destroyed by wear alone), but every repair *attempt* — field or workbench — carries a tunable failure chance that destroys the item permanently instead of restoring it.
