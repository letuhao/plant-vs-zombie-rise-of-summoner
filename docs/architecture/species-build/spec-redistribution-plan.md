# Spec: `redistribution-plan`

Module 4 in the [species-build capability map](../species-build-map.md). **No dependencies.** The one
genuinely new mechanism in this program; everything else is wiring.

## Objective

Turn each species' classified **build favour** into a full **share vector** over the twelve aptitudes,
computed deterministically at generation time, such that the corpus-wide distribution of allocated
points lands inside a tunable **parity band**.

Six owner decisions define it, and they are restated here rather than linked because a downstream
session reads this file:

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| 3 | A **deterministic function** plans the redistribution. **No single-primary vectors** — a one-stat unit is simultaneously too strong on its axis and too weak everywhere else |
| 6 | **The LLM does not do balance.** It classified a category; the distribution is arithmetic |
| 7 | **Generation time, shipped as static knowledge** — a species' build is learned once and stays true. Not a per-run roll |
| 8 | The objective is **distribution parity**, explicitly *not* a win-rate claim |
| 11 | Parity is measured over **total allocated points**, never over the primary field — the function steers *remainders* and never overrides its own input |
| 12 | The target is a **band** (tunable floor and ceiling), not minimised deviation |
| 16 | The **per-species lean varies** — crowded primaries lean less, rare primaries lean more. The lean **falls out of solving for the band**; it is not a separate knob |

**Why it exists, measured:** across the 840-row corpus, `Onslaught` is the classified primary of
**39.5%** of species while `Vigor`, `Might`, `Composure` and `Ferocity` share **2.3%** between them, and
**79.3%** of species are `pure` (a 100/0 favour). Allocating straight down that favour would mass-produce
corner builds and hand 133 species the corner the class-system has already *measured* as beating all
eleven others.

**Success looks like:** a checked-in, diffable, `--check`-regenerable plan whose band satisfaction is a
**pass/fail assertion**, not a report.

## Design

### Shape

One canonical generated file, not 829 edits:

```
data/generated/demons/_species-build-plan.json
```

Underscore-prefixed, so the existing anchor readers skip it by their own established convention
(`DemonSpeciesGen`/`DemonSpeciesImport` both skip `_`-prefixed files). Extending the per-species files
instead would move all 829 of them and couple this plan to `SpeciesExpander`'s output, which
**decision 5 explicitly keeps separate** — base stats are not regenerated by this program.

Each entry is `speciesId → { aptitudeId: sharePermille }`, shares summing to exactly **1000**.

### The algorithm — closed-form, not a search

Three phases. **No iterate-until-converged loop**, because a loop that might not converge is a loop
that ships an out-of-band plan on a bad day.

**Phase 1 — derive each species' lean from its primary's crowding.** This is decision 16, made
concrete. For species *i* with primary *P*:

```
crowding(P)  = primaryCount(P) / speciesCount          // permille
lean_i       = clamp(leanMaxPermille - crowdingFactor × crowding(P), leanMinPermille, leanMaxPermille)
```

A crowded primary (Onslaught, 395‰) yields a lean near `leanMin`; a rare one (Ferocity, 2‰) yields a
lean near `leanMax`. **This is the whole of "common archetypes are generalists, rare archetypes are
specialists"** — one formula, no per-species authoring, and it is why the lean is not a tunable of its
own.

**Phase 2 — distribute the remainder against the running corpus deficit.** Iterating species in
**ordinal `speciesId` order** (reproducibility, matching `ZombossPatterns`' and `DemonSpeciesGen`'s own
convention):

- The primary receives `lean_i`.
- A non-`none` secondary receives `secondarySharePermille` of the remainder.
- What is left is assigned to the aptitudes with the **largest current deficit** against the band floor,
  in deficit order with an ordinal tiebreak, up to `maxAptitudesPerSpecies`.

Because the corpus deficit is recomputed as the pass proceeds, early species fill the rarest aptitudes
and later ones spread wider — deterministic, single-pass, and terminating by construction.

**Phase 3 — verify, and refuse.** Compute each aptitude's corpus-wide share. If any falls outside
`[floorPermille, ceilingPermille]`, the tool **exits non-zero and names the aptitudes and their shares**.
It never writes an out-of-band plan and never silently relaxes the band.

### The feasibility constraint is real and must be checked, not assumed

Audit finding A7: if the lean were **uniform**, Onslaught's corpus floor would be `0.395 × lean` no
matter how perfectly remainders are steered — so a 15% ceiling would require a lean ≤ 38%. Phase 1's
per-species lean is exactly what dissolves that. **But the band is still not satisfiable for arbitrary
tunables**, and Phase 3 is the thing that says so out loud instead of shipping a near-miss.

### Tunables

All in `data/tuning/species-build.v1.json`; a missing key is a **load rejection naming it**, never a
default:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `parityFloorPermille` / `parityCeilingPermille` | the band, decision 12 |
| `leanMinPermille` / `leanMaxPermille` | the ends of the per-species lean range |
| `crowdingFactor` | how hard crowding pushes the lean down |
| `secondarySharePermille` | what a non-`none` secondary takes of the remainder |
| `maxAptitudesPerSpecies` | keeps a vector legible rather than a smear across twelve |
| `minAptitudesPerSpecies` | **≥ 2 — this is decision 3's "no single-primary" made mechanical** |

**`secondarySharePermille` is its own key and deliberately does not reuse `demon-shape.v1.json`'s
`impureSecondaryShareMilli: 300`.** That constant governs **base stat** generation, which decision 5
keeps untouched; sharing it would couple two layers that were deliberately separated.

### Numeric rules

Permille `long` throughout. **Widen before multiplying, divide by 1000 last, exactly once.** Shares are
bounded ratios and say so in a comment (exempt from PS-8). Rounding uses the **largest-remainder
method with an ordinal tiebreak** so a vector always sums to exactly 1000 and the same input always
produces the same bytes. No `float` anywhere.

## Project structure

```

```

The tool mirrors `DemonSpeciesGen`'s existing shape exactly — same flags, same `--check` semantics,
same `_`-prefix skipping, same "refuse the whole thing rather than write half" discipline.

## Code style

- `SpeciesBuildPlanner` is **pure and Core-only**: no file IO, no `RpgStore`, no model calls ever. The
  tool reads and writes; the planner computes. This is `SpeciesExpander`'s own division and it is why
  the planner is testable without a corpus on disk.
- Ordinal ordering everywhere a collection is iterated or serialized.
- Every band/lean number comes from tuning; the planner holds **no bare literal** — it is a Math/Policy
  file and therefore the balance surface (`tunables-ssot.md`).
- The canonical serializer sorts keys and pins formatting so a rerun is byte-identical.

## Testing strategy

1. **Determinism:** two runs over the same corpus produce byte-identical output. Also that a shuffled
   input order produces the *same* plan — ordering is by `speciesId`, not by file discovery.
2. **No single-primary:** every emitted vector has at least `minAptitudesPerSpecies` non-zero entries.
   A synthetic corpus of all-`pure` species must still satisfy it.
3. **Vectors sum to exactly 1000**, including with awkward remainders — the largest-remainder property.
4. **Band satisfaction on the real corpus** — the acceptance test, and it is pass/fail.
5. **Refusal:** given deliberately infeasible tunables (e.g. a ceiling below what the primaries alone
   force), the tool **exits non-zero and names the offending aptitudes**. This is the test that stops a
   near-miss from shipping.
6. **Crowding behaves:** in a synthetic corpus where one aptitude is over-represented, its species get a
   measurably lower lean than a rare-primary species. Decision 16, asserted rather than assumed.
7. **The favour is never overridden** (decision 11): every species' highest share is still its
   classified primary. If that ever fails, the function has started reassigning primaries, which is
   explicitly out of scope.
8. **Overflow:** an extreme corpus throws rather than wraps.

## Boundaries

- **Always:** fail loudly rather than emit an out-of-band plan; iterate ordinally; keep the planner pure;
  keep every band/lean number in tuning.
- **Ask first:** the band values themselves (a balance pass owns them — shipping a guess is fine,
  calling it balance is not); adding a runtime component; letting the planner read anything player-scoped.
- **Never:** reassign a species' primary; regenerate base stats (decision 5); call a model; use `float`;
  emit a plan that fails Phase 3; introduce randomness — a random baseline is unlearnable, which is
  exactly why decision 7 declined Dragon Quest Monsters' growth-spurt precedent.

## Success criteria

1. `--check` is clean and byte-stable on a rerun over the real corpus.
2. The band is satisfied on the real corpus, asserted pass/fail.
3. Every vector is multi-aptitude and sums to 1000; every vector's top share is its classified primary.
4. Infeasible tunables produce a named refusal, not a near-miss.
5. Crowded-primary species demonstrably lean less than rare-primary ones.
6. `audit-magic-numbers` finds no bare literal in the planner; `audit-overflow` clean.
