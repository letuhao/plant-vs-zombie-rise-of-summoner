## F1 — `battle-magnitude` produces a negative coefficient

**The claim under attack:** *"Per-channel `C` and `A` cost two extra tuning rows and preserve the
property that matters: `B` is still the only dial."* (`spec-battle-magnitude.md` §2.1)

**The arithmetic.** With a shared *absolute* `B = 0.4` and each channel pinned at Θ=20:

| Channel | `C` | pin | derived `A` |
|---|---|---|---|
| hp | 80 | 680 | 26.200 |
| atk | 12 | 92 | 0.200 |
| defense | 2 | 22 | **−2.800** |

The quadratic term at the pin is `0.4 × 190 = 76`. Defense's entire pin value is 22. The solver has
to produce a **negative slope** to hit the pin, so `BaseDefense` would *decrease* with level and go
negative almost immediately. Atk survives only by luck, at `A = 0.2` — a near-flat curve that is
plainly not the intent either.

**Debate.** *Could the pin move for small channels instead?* No — the pins are read from shipped code
(`12 + 4·20 = 92`, `2 + 20 = 22`), and moving them is the golden movement wave 2 exists to avoid.
*Could defense simply not take the dial?* That reintroduces exactly the independent-curve drift the
program exists to end (SSOT §0).

**Adjudication.** The error is treating `B` as an absolute increment shared across channels of wildly
different scale. **`B` is a shape parameter, and must be applied proportionally:**

```text
B_ch = B × pinValue_ch / pinValue_hp
```

| Channel | `B_ch` | `A` | `P(100)` | vs linear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hp | 0.40000 | 26.2000 | 4,680 | ×1.52 |
| atk | 0.05412 | 3.4859 | 628 | ×1.53 |
| defense | 0.01294 | 0.8771 | 154 | ×1.51 |

All positive, and all three grow by the **same ratio** — which is the property §2.1 wanted and did
not achieve. `B` remains one dial.

## F2 — the contest gap diverges, which would have ended the grind in practice

> **Endless grind is an owner decision and is not under audit here.** It is the SSOT that other
> systems reconcile *to*. What is under audit is a mechanical claim this document made *in service
> of* it — and the defect matters precisely because it would have broken the thing it was meant to
> deliver.

**The claim under attack:** *"`realmsAdvanced` is unbounded **and it raises `Θ_content` too** … So the
*difference* that decides contests stays bounded while both sides climb forever."* (SSOT §5.1)

**Why it matters.** If the gap diverges, then past some world count every contest is decided before
it starts: statuses always land, hits always connect, content stops resisting. The numbers keep
rising and the *game* stops. That is a grind that has ended in every sense except the arithmetic —
which is the failure mode endless-grind design exists to avoid.

**The arithmetic.** At the §5.3 starting weights, retiring a world gives the actor `Wa = 25` and the
content `Ww = 5` (one size tier):

| After world | Gap |
|---|---|
| 1 | +20 |
| 3 | +60 |
| 7 | +140 |

The gap grows **+20 Θ per world, forever.** Both sides climb, but not together — the player outruns
content by 5×. Within a handful of worlds every contest is decided before it starts.

**Debate.** *Is Fracture intensity the missing term?* It is real (`empire-economy-ssot.md` §4) but it
is not in `Θ_content` — the SSOT's own §5 composition has only `Wz·zombossLevel + Wm·mapLevel +
Ww·worldTier`. Citing an escalation the formula does not contain is precisely the "a comment is not
evidence" failure the design gate warns about. *Is `Wz·zombossLevel` the term?* Only if Zomboss levels
once per world, which nothing states.

**Adjudication.** Endless grind requires that content climbs *with* the player, so the claim holds
only if **content gains per world equal actor gains per world**. Two ways to get there, and the
second is better:

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| A — set `Ww = Wa = 25` | Mechanical. But it overloads world *size* with world *difficulty*, and the tiers are ~8→128 nodes, a different axis |
| **B — add an explicit `Wf · realmsAdvanced` term to `Θ_content`** | The escalation the prose already claims, written into the formula. `Wf = Wa` makes the gap **exactly constant**; `Wf` slightly under `Wa` makes the player gain ground slowly, which is what a prestige loop should feel like |

**Adopted: B, with `Wf = Wa = 25`** — see F8, which caught the first answer to this.

## F8 — the fix for F2 did not hold either

**The claim under attack:** F2's own first adjudication, *"`Wf = 20` against `Wa = 25` … felt, but not
runaway."*

**The arithmetic.** `Wa − Wf = 5`, so the gap still grows **+5 Θ per world, forever** — slower
divergence is still divergence:

| Worlds | Gap | σ(gap/100) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 50 | 0.62 |
| 50 | 250 | 0.92 |
| **100** | **500** | **0.993 — saturated** |
| 500 | 2,500 | 1.0000 |

The gap holds at a constant **20% of `Θ_actor`**, which is what made it look bounded. But a sigmoid
reads the *absolute* difference, and it saturates near world 60–100. Past that every contest is
decided before it starts — precisely the F2 failure, arriving later.

**"Bounded" was the wrong word twice.** The first time it was asserted without subtracting two rates;
the second time it was checked as a ratio when the consuming formula reads a difference.

**"Bounded gap" is not "bounded progression".** Both are required and they are different axes: `Θ`
climbs forever on both sides (endless), while the *difference* between them stays flat (still a
contest). Conflating the two is what produced the wrong word.

**Debate.** *Then `Wf = Wa` gives zero progression per world — does a prestige loop with no advantage
feel like anything?* It does, because **the contest gap is not where a player's advantage lives.**
SSOT §4.5 already says power grows on three multiplied axes: magnitude, **breadth** (affixes, sockets,
tiers) and **roster** (demons, fusions, patron). Content has a `Θ` and nothing else — no gear, no
roster, no build. The player outpaces content on the two axes content cannot have, while the `Θ`
contest stays a fair fight at every depth.

That is a better design than the one F2 first proposed: escalation the player *feels* without an
arithmetic advantage that eventually deletes the challenge.

**Adjudication. `Wf = Wa = 25`, and it is an invariant, not a weight** — `Wf ≠ Wa` is rejected at
load. Progression per world is delivered by breadth and roster, which are outside `Θ` by design.

## F3 — the theorem is inverted under a power-scaled divisor

**The claim under attack:** *"In a difference-based contest, the power curve must be linear in
level."* (SSOT §2, stated as **the** governing theorem — *"This theorem is the whole document"*)

**The arithmetic.** A sigmoid contest is `σ((a−b)/s)`. Everything depends on whether `s` is constant:

| Divisor | Curve | Gap 5 at Θ=10 → Θ=10,000 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| constant `/100` | linear | 0.5125 → 0.5125 | **invariant** |
| constant `/100` | geometric | 0.6548 → 1.0000 | **explodes** — theorem holds |
| `K × matchPower` | linear | 0.5010 → **0.5000** | **drifts — the gap loses value** |
| `K × matchPower` | geometric | 0.5017 → 0.5017 | **invariant** |

The bottom two rows are the theorem's exact inverse. Under a power-scaled divisor, **linear is the
wrong choice**: a fixed advantage becomes worth less and less until, at high Θ, every status apply is
a coin flip regardless of how far ahead you are.

Both regimes ship today. `CombatPolicies.*Scale = 100.0` is constant (battle rates);
`effectiveApplyScale = ApplyScaleK × matchPower` is scaled (`ResistanceEvaluator.cs:150-152`).

**This contradicts the SSOT internally.** §6.1 already observed the cancellation — *"dividing by
matchPower silently converts the apply roll into a ratio contest"* — and then §2 asserts the theorem
universally anyway. The audit's own document held both halves and did not notice.

**Debate.** *Keep both regimes and pick the curve per regime?* That is two power systems wearing one
name, and it is unreasonable to ask any future contributor to track which contest is which. *Make
everything a ratio contest?* Then a level-1 actor and a level-10,000 actor with the same *proportional*
advantage fight identically — defensible, but it discards the level gap as a meaningful quantity and
would move every rate golden.

**Adjudication. One regime everywhere: constant divisor, linear curve, difference contest.** Remove
`× matchPower` from `effectiveApplyScale`. Then §2's theorem is true *by construction* instead of by
accident, and both halves of the evaluator finally agree about what power means.

**Consequence: `spec-status-contest.md` §2.4 was wrong to defer this.** It argued the divisor split
*"stops being harmful under a linear `Θ`."* F3 shows the opposite — under linear `Θ` the scaled
divisor introduces a *new* failure. The divisor fix moves into `status-contest` as change #4.

## F4 — `netFactor` is an unbounded multiplier with a cliff at gap 2

**The claim under attack:** `spec-status-contest.md` §2.3's before/after table, which shows a matched
pair yielding base magnitude at every Θ and concludes the fix is complete.

**The arithmetic.** The table only tested **matched** pairs. Mismatched:

| Gap (Θ) | `netFactor` | magnitude from base 20 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.0 | 20 |
| 1 | 1.0 | 20 |
| **2** | **2.0** | **40** |
| 5 | 5.0 | 100 |
| 25 | 25.0 | 500 |

`netFactor = clamp(delta, 0, 10000)` (`ResistanceEvaluator.cs:216`) is a raw difference used directly
as a **multiplier**, with the shipped even-match special case pinning `delta=0 → 1.0`. So parity and
+1 both give 1.0×, and +2 abruptly gives 2.0×. **A one-index step doubles status potency at the
cliff**, and one retired world (`Wa=25`) gives 25×.

The old stub hid this the same way it hid §6: at `tierPower = 1.0` the gap was always ~1.

**Debate.** *Is this in scope?* The formula predates the ladder, so arguably not. But the ladder is
what makes the gap *reachable* — before, `delta` was pinned near 1 by the stub, so the shape was never
exercised. Shipping a correct curve into a formula that cannot absorb it is not a fix.

**Adjudication.** `netFactor` must be normalized like every other contest read:

```text
netFactor = 1 + delta / NetFactorScale          # NetFactorScale a tuning constant, ~10
```

Parity gives exactly 1.0 with no special case; a 10-index gap gives 2.0×; the cliff disappears. The
shipped `delta == 0 → 1.0` branch becomes redundant and is deleted rather than left as a trap.
`MaxNetFactor` stays as a ceiling.

## F5 — `content-scale` violates PS-7 in the document that declares it

`spec-content-scale.md` §2.1 writes `contentScale = PowerLadder.Value(Θc) / 680`, and its Boundaries
say *"Ask first — changing the 680 denominator."* PS-7 says **every number in this document is data**.
`680` is `pinValue`, and it is already in the tuning file.

**Adjudication.** Read `pinValue` from tuning. The boundary line becomes redundant and is removed. A
small finding, but it is the standard failing at its first contact with a real spec — which is
exactly when standards fail.

## F6 — `power-guard`'s G3 is not implementable as written

*"G3 — inventory closed: fails when a power-shaped scale exists that SSOT §10 does not list."* A
scanner cannot decide what is "power-shaped" in general; that judgement is what the A3 triage
(`power-plan.md` P0.3) exists for.

**Adjudication.** Scope G3 to what a scanner can actually do: **flag any *new* `f(level)`-shaped
declaration not present in `inventory.json`**, i.e. a diff against a known baseline rather than a
semantic judgement. That is genuinely enforceable and catches the real regression — someone adding a
curve — while not pretending to classify existing code.

## F7 — the `BattleSetup` rename moves four hashes

`spec-content-authoring.md` proposes `BattleSetup.Level → Index` with an alias escape hatch.

**Adjudication.** The rename is **internal only** — the serialized name stays. The escape hatch

## Round 2 — `caps-reconcile`, written after round 1

### F10 — the PvZ drop caps are not in code

`spec-caps-reconcile.md` §2.3 lists *"PvZ item drops, 2/run and 12/day — **Deleted**"* alongside three
real code deletions, and its Structure block names **no file for it**.

There is none. `ssot-generation.md:827` specifies the rule (*"PvZ run — 0.50, hard cap 2/run and
12/day"*), and item drop generation is **unbuilt** — there is no `src/FusionRpg.Core/Items`. Grepping
for the caps in `src/` returns nothing.

**Adjudication.** It joins enhancement and rarity promotion as a **design reconciliation on an unbuilt
feature**, not a code change. Cheap now, a rebalance later. Moved out of §2.3's deletion table.

### F11 — a daily throttle that every sweep missed

`SoulEarnPolicy.VictoryFullPerDay = 3` (`SoulEarnPolicy.cs:17`), consumed at `:59`:

```csharp
priorVictoriesToday < VictoryFullPerDay ? VictoryDelta : VictoryDelta / 2
```

Victory pays +100 souls, **full for three wins a day, then halved.** That is a wall-clock pacing
mechanism in a single-player game with no monetization — precisely the defect the owner rejected for
PvZ drops, and precisely what `standalone-rpg-map.md` already ruled against for stamina.

**Three cap sweeps missed it**, and the reason is worth recording: it is named
`VictoryFullPerDay`, not `Max` / `Cap` / `Limit` / `Ceil`. Every sweep grepped the ceiling vocabulary
and this one describes a *threshold*. It is also not a ceiling in form — nothing is refused, the rate
just halves — so even a semantic reading of the constant alone would not flag it. It only shows up by
reading the **consumer**.

> **Lesson for PS-8: a cap is a shape, not a word.** `VictoryFullPerDay` caps nothing and throttles
> everything. The audit tools grep declarations; this one needed its use site. Recorded as a known
> blind spot rather than pretended away.

**Adjudication.** Deleted with the other soul caps, under the same §11.7 reasoning and the same
condition — the scaling earn formula lands with it.

### F12 — `MaxSoulAward` is a different kind of bound

The spec derives all three ceilings the same way: *"the largest value that path's arithmetic can carry
without overflowing."* That is right for `ShieldMath.MaxInput` and `ResourceDeltaMath.AmountCap`, which
bound a **single computation**.

It is wrong for `MaxSoulAward`, whose own comment states a different constraint: *"keeps SQLite
integer addition far from 64-bit overflow, which silently degrades to REAL and permanently corrupts
the snapshot."* The danger is the **accumulated balance**, not one award. Deriving from the award
path's arithmetic yields something near `int64` — technically correct, operationally useless, because
the thing that overflows is `balance + award` after ten thousand awards.

**Adjudication.** Two different obligations:

| Bound | Derive from | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| `ShieldMath.MaxInput`, `ResourceDeltaMath.AmountCap` | the widest intermediate of **one** computation | that computation would overflow |
| `MaxSoulAward` | the **balance headroom**: `int64Max − currentBalance` | the *ledger* would overflow |

The soul bound is therefore **dynamic, not a constant** — and checking it against the live balance is
also what makes it a real guarantee rather than a guess about how rich a player will get.

### F13 — a derived bound can depend on another cap

`ShieldMath`'s widest intermediate involves penetration, and penetration is itself bounded by
`ShieldPolicy.PenCapKPm = 3000`. So the "derived" bound is a function of **other caps**, not of the
path alone — and if two bounds ever reference each other the derivation has no fixed point.

**Adjudication.** Not circular today, but the spec must say so rather than assume it: each derived
bound declares which other constants it reads, and a test asserts the dependency graph is acyclic.
One line of obligation now, against a class of bug that is very hard to see later.

## What this audit says about the specs generally

Three of the four critical findings share one root: **a claim was tested only in the case that made
it look right.**

- F1 checked hp, not defense.
- F2 asserted both sides climb without subtracting the two rates.
- F4 tabulated matched pairs and skipped mismatched ones.

F3 is different and worse — the SSOT contained both halves of its own contradiction, in §2 and §6.1,
and stated one of them as *"the whole document."*

**F8 is the one worth remembering.** It is a defect in this audit's own remedy, found by running the
same attack on the fix that had just been written. "Bounded" was wrong twice: first asserted without
subtracting two rates, then verified as a *ratio* when the consuming sigmoid reads a *difference*.
A fix deserves the same arithmetic as the thing it fixes, and the second pass is where the subtler
error lives.

**The cheap defence is arithmetic on the adversarial case**, and it took under a minute per finding
once the question was asked. Every fix above is now carried by a test in its module's spec, so the
next person to break one of these gets a red suite rather than a document to re-read.
