## 1. On replacing a damage matrix with additive bonuses

**Dustin Browder**, lead designer, StarCraft II — GameStar, 2010-03-25. *The single most useful quote
in the corpus.*

> **Q:** *"In the initial Starcraft certain units were doing less damage to certain other units. Now
> there is a specific amount of bonus damage. Why did you change that system?"*
>
> **Browder:** *"In the original Starcraft there were different weapon types that did 100 percent
> damage to certain unit types, 75 percent damage to other unit types and 50 percent damage to other
> unit types. The units were divided into small, medium and large. **We wanted to make that system a
> lot more transparent and obvious. Before, you had to be a hardcore player or surf the web to
> understand how the system worked.** In Starcraft 2, we put the damage modifiers in the user
> interface and simplified them a little more. So instead of doing 50 or 75 or 100 percent damage, we
> added a single damage bonus against certain unit types. **That makes it more clear to the player
> which units to use against which other units.**"*

⭐ **He mis-states SC1's own numbers in that quote** — he says 100/75/50; the real table is 100/50/25
(Concussive) and 50/75/100 (Explosive). **The lead designer of the sequel got the source system wrong
in an interview about how confusing it was.** That is evidence, not anecdote.

**Kicho**, Blizzard Korea, 2009-01-14 — the same change, from the other side:

> *"**Damage calculation is also different from the previous game. Units' attacks always do at least
> 100% of the damage value shown on the screen.** And then, depending on the attack's property,
> additional damage may be dealt when attacking units with specific attributes. … **If the old system
> of 'explosive, concussive' and 'small, medium, large' was used, then Marines would take reduced
> damage from a Siege Tank.**"*

**They removed the *penalty* cells**, because the printed damage number stopped being trustworthy.

**Browder on removing high-ground miss chance**, same era:
> *"In SC1, there was a chance that units on the low ground would miss enemies on high ground. **We
> removed this percentage since we do not like chance elements. The players ought to know exactly what
> advantage they have. And how to counter it.**"*

## 2. On counter strength — qualitative only, never a number

**Browder**, GameStar 2010:
> *"We've also heard concerns about Starcraft 2 being **a little too hard countered with a lot of
> systems not being muddy enough**. … **So we think it is primarily a ratio issue which we are going
> to adjust.**"*
>
> *"Starcraft is a game of hard counters and of muddy counters. … **So the philosophy for Starcraft is
> that there is no philosophy for Starcraft.**"*

**Blizzard, LotV Multiplayer Development Update, 2015-02-12** — removing a bonus term *because* it
created a hard counter:
> *"**Since we still don't want Tempests to hard counter units like Brood Lords, Carriers, or
> Battlecruisers we're looking to remove the +massive damage component** and then add Disintegration."*

**Browder**, Game Informer 2010 — counters must be micro-overrideable:
> *"we do have some triangles in the game, but we also try to make sure that a lot of the
> relationships … **can be changed fundamentally with micro.** For example, **Zealots counter Stalkers
> unless the Stalkers kite, and if the Stalkers kite the Zealots will lose.** … **It's not enough to
> just build the right units; it also matters how you use that unit.**"*

And on counter legibility as a *feel* goal:
> *"when you see an enemy unit, you know immediately, 'I know exactly what I have to do to stop that,
> I have got the perfect unit that's going to roll him.' … **That kind of precision and crispness
> really makes the game feel great.**"*

**Rob Pardo**, GDC 2008 "Rules of Engagement" — session notes:
> - **"Every unit has a counter"**
> - **"Math is the foundation"** · **"Everything should feel overpowered"** · **"Don't balance the game into mediocrity"**
> - **"Avoid hidden modifiers — do this very selectively"**
> - **"StarCraft II — 15 units is the right amount, depth to game, learn units and strategies, can still easily identify units, understand counterstrategy"**

## 3. ⭐ On roster size and redundancy

**Browder**, Game Informer 2010 — *the best passage in the corpus on unit redundancy*:

> *"**We had a lot more units in the game. We sometimes had fifteen or sixteen units on the Zerg or
> Protoss at any given moment before we finally got down to 14 now. Sometimes as many as 17 or 18 on
> the Zerg, which meant that the race matchups got very confusing.** It was difficult to know what to
> build at any given moment, and there was lots of overlap either between the units on a single race
> or between the units on a race and another unit. **You'd play as the Zerg with something called a
> Spore Beast and we'd say, 'Oh my god, this is just a Banshee, isn't it?' We'd try to tune [it] as
> like a really *fast* Banshee, and it was like, 'Okay, dude, but that's still a Banshee.' It hadn't
> fundamentally changed its role.**"*
>
> *"[the Queen] was trying to be an offensive caster, and she was trying to be a defensive caster at
> the same time. **She was a little conflicted as to what she was supposed to accomplish.** … **the
> Thor was sort of wandering around, trying to figure out what his role on the battlefield was.**"*
>
> On the Thor's original AoE cannons: *"**It stepped on the Siege Tank in the worst way. You really
> didn't need a Siege Tank if you had a Thor, or if you had a Siege Tank you didn't need a Thor.**"*

**Browder**, GameStar 2010:
> *"We didn't want to have 20 units per faction because that would be too much to memorize."*
>
> *"**Our goal for Starcraft is not having the maximum number of units possible, but the minimum. We
> are not trying to make the biggest game, we are trying to make the smallest, best game.**"*
>
> *"For example, **the Archon was on the chopping block for a long time.**"*

**Browder**, Blizzard retrospective 2018-03-04:
> *"**You don't want a 'Marine 1' and 'Marine 2' scenario. That would just be a waste, because you'd
> just end up picking the 'best' one.**"*

**David Kim**, same piece, on the Dragoon: *"**It was too good at too many things.**"* And:
*"Eventually, we decided that **if we do bring something back, we'll have to cut something else.**"*

**Browder** on the Reaper:
> *"**The Reaper doesn't have a core combat role.** He can't replace the Marine, he can't replace the
> Marauder. **He's very close, right now in Wings, to a Hellion. So we had to make a couple of
> choices: Do we want the Reaper to be a better Hellion, or do we want the Hellion to be a better
> Reaper?**"*

**Browder**, April 2012 dev update, on the Warhound before it was cut:
> *"**The warhound still feels a little too much like a small thor, which is just not new enough to
> give terran players new strategies.**"*

Blizzard's post-mortem, 2013:
> *"**The Warhound was removed very early in the Heart of the Swarm public beta test because we felt
> that its role overlapped with the Marauder a bit too much.** Mechanically, these two units were
> virtually identical, but were produced from two different buildings."*

**Note the two official reasons differ and both are role-redundancy reasons** — "too much like a small
Thor" before, "overlapped with the Marauder" after. **Neither is a numbers complaint.**

## 4. ⭐ On removing, and re-adding, a counter matrix

**Toshiyuki Kusakihara**, director, Intelligent Systems — why *Three Houses* **removed** the weapon
triangle:

> *"We think that the weapon triangle is somewhat of a stylized system, **it isn't really realistic**.
> If you have a situation where a novice axe user takes down an advanced lance user, well, that makes
> sense? Probably not. So, we wanted to make something that comes across as more realistic to warfare
> and have players develop their weapons skills individually."*

**The objection is that a categorical counter overrides accumulated investment.**

**Genki Yokota**, Nintendo producer, on the same removal:
> *"The weapon triangle system hasn't disappeared, **it's just less visible.** … What we wanted to do
> in Three Houses is to give player freedom in tutoring their students…"*

**Hayato Iwata**, director, Omega Force / Koei Tecmo — why *Three Hopes* **restored** it:

> *"**We thought that simple visual cues make for better choices and gameplay.** Although not in the
> original game (Three Houses) the ideas of weapon efficacy and compatibility did exist in the Fire
> Emblem series, and **we thought players should be able to understand the concept fairly easily as it
> is not an entirely new concept to them.**"*
>
> *"We originally gave each class a single weapon and decided what weapons would be effective against
> what based on that, but **we ultimately went with the weapon triangles.**"*

**Hayashi**, same interview: *"We didn't use that system in the beginning and **felt that the gameplay
wasn't as interesting.**"*

**Nintendo's own editorial framing** (Ask the Developer Vol. 8, Engage) treats it as series identity,
not an optimum:
> *"A system much like the triangle hierarchy in a rock-paper-scissors game… **The weapons triangle is
> a gameplay feature passed down from past games as one of the characteristics of the Fire Emblem
> series.**"*

⚠️ Kusakihara and Yokota quotes verified only against their English republishers; GameSpot and
Jeuxvideo returned HTTP 403.

## 5. On adding a type to fix a broken one

**Junichi Masuda**, Pokémon, on the Fairy type:
> *"**The Dragon type was so strong. It was difficult no matter how we tinkered with it… It wouldn't
> change. And it would have been sad to make the Dragon type weaker.**"*

So they added a type instead. Elsewhere he notes they *"added a new type that is strong against
Dragons"* because *"we saw little deviation in the Pokémon that were used, even at tournaments"*, and
that the addition triggered a full rebalance and internal tournaments. **Fairy is immune to Dragon** —
the strongest possible intervention.

**Masuda on complexity generally:**
> *"Personally, I don't like it when things get too complicated. I want Pokémon games to be accessible
> and approachable."*
> *"We think that the balance is one of the most important elements… **That's the only way — to
> actually play and make sure that the battle is balanced.**"*

**⭐ Masuda on Dexit — the clearest "our roster outgrew our balance capacity" statement in the genre:**
> *"There are a couple of different parts to the thinking behind it, but really the biggest reason for
> it is just **the sheer number of Pokémon. We already have well over 800 Pokémon species.**"*
> *"In the end I had no choice but to prioritize quality."*

Famitsu's framing was blunter: the total exceeded 1,000 including forms, and *"it became very
difficult to adjust the balance of each Pokémon's unique skills."*

**Ken Sugimori**, art director, at 650 species — running the other way:
> *"It's not only myself designing new Pokémon—we have about 20 people in our team at Game Freak who
> design Pokémon, so if every one of them came up with 10 Pokémon ideas, it's going to be 200."*

## 6. On elements and reactions

**Genshin Impact spokesperson**, IGN 2022, on why Dendro took 23 months:
> *"The Dendro element is **the last piece of the puzzle in the seven-element system.** … However,
> **at the time of design, we were unable to come up with a mechanic that the development team found
> satisfying and innovative.** … We made a deeper connection between the Dendro element and
> Pyro/Hydro/Electro… **which is also the reason why we released the Dendro element along with its
> featured nation.**"*

**An element without a good reaction was worth *not shipping*.**

**HoYoverse**, official Behind the Scenes video, on the reaction system:
> *"The design of the Elemental Reaction system was **established long before combat strategy and
> numerical planning.** It was designed to support gameplay interaction, forming a set of **modular,
> intuitive, and easy-to-understand rules** for players to engage with."*

**Reactions were the foundation; the numbers were fitted afterwards.**

**HoYoverse**, official Zhongli adjustment post, quoting community feedback back:
> *"In parties with Geo Resonance effects, the support that Zhongli provides is less pronounced than
> other available Geo characters, giving him relatively low strategic value, **especially considering
> that he is a 5-star character.**"*

The announcement had a section headed **"● Regarding Geo Elemental Mechanics"** — HoYoverse framed the
problem as *partly element-level*, not only character-level.

## 7. On power creep

**Honkai: Star Rail development team**, written statement to Polygon, Oct 2023 — full text:
> 1. **Balancing stats:** *"we make adjustments to characters and equipment to ensure that new
>    characters and equipment are not too overpowered."*
> 2. **New mechanics and content:** *"we introduce new game mechanics and content in real-time…
>    **players can try out different strategies and permutations rather than merely relying on
>    numerical advantage.**"*
> 3. *"We also keep in close contact with our player base…"*

**Chengnan An**, HSR lead designer, GDC 2025:
> *"If we're just thinking about the combat system when we're designing characters, then we ran out of
> inspo really, really fast. **So we try not to use that approach for a lot of the characters.**"*
>
> On characters that miss: *"**we didn't waste time thinking about it. We would just go into creating
> the next character.**"*
>
> On mechanic-level creep: *"**The new combat systems are not substitutes for the older ones**, and
> there is no absolute which one is better or which one is weaker."*

**Smilegate**, Epic Seven Balance Adjustment Direction Notice — *the only first-party use of the term
found*:
> *"Our Balance Team's main priority has been **aiming to curb power-creep since the game's launch.**"*

## 8. On surfaces, determinism and gates

**Nick Pechenin**, systems designer, Larian — *the most useful design quote in the corpus for
generated content*:
> *"It was also important to us that **although the ways in which surfaces are created and interact
> with each other have almost no randomness, smallest deviations in how the player targets their
> skills and positions their characters lead to wildly divergent outcomes**, essentially generating
> fresh combat experiences every time."*

**Deterministic rules + positional sensitivity = variety without RNG.**

**Pechenin**, on the AI teaching the system:
> *"In Original Sin 2, the AI will always know what will happen when it throws a fireball at an oil
> barrel. … **the AI started teaching players new mechanics.** Even if you didn't know anything about
> the surfaces… **You could learn just by watching.**"*

**⭐ Swen Vincke**, Larian, on the armour system's failure — *the sharpest gate warning found*:
> *"Our combat designers had to remove crowd control from the AI, because **it focused on one
> character, made sure to destroy their physical and magical armour, and then would start to control
> it, then kill it.** That wasn't fun because one guy was dealing with everything, but **it was a
> dominant tactic.** So we had to nerf it a lot."*

**Larian abandoned the system in 2026.** Pechenin's replacement criterion:
> *"There will be ways to protect your characters from harm, but **you will not have to wait before
> you can use your fun skills on enemies.**"*

## 9. On the colour pie as a permission system

**Mark Rosewater**, Magic: The Gathering:
> *"The color pie not only tells colors what they can do, it also tells them what they cannot. **This
> built-in weakness into each color is core to the game.**"*
>
> *"the times that R&D has had the greatest problems is when we allow colors to get access to things
> they're not supposed to have."*
>
> On why five: *"It's a matter of each color having enough, but their being a large enough volume of
> things to go around. **An odd number helps keep it balanced — colors have the same numbers of allies
> as enemies.**"*
>
> ⭐ *"**The main purpose of the color pie is to make colors feel and play differently. It is not the
> best tool for balancing power.**"*

## 10. On process — playtesting over analysis

**Blizzard**, LotV update 2014-11-19:
> *"**It's really not possible to definitively say how good or bad a change is until we've had a lot
> of playtesting.** Recently we've seen some feedback and discussion turn into definitive conclusions
> on certain subjects, **many of which differ from what we're seeing in our internal playtesting.**"*

**Browder**, Game Informer 2010 — on whether unit mobility was a design goal:
> *"**No. … We didn't set out with any goals in mind**, and I'm sure that this will upset the fans
> terribly. What we did instead was that we said, '**We want to make a bunch of cool units, and we're
> going to make each unit as cool as we can possibly make it, and then we'll see how it all works
> together, and we'll tune as necessary from there.**'"*

**Browder** on cost as a sanity check, not an input:
> *"You try to create really solid tools. … you have to look at a unit and say, '**You know, for its
> cost, that unit has uses. That could be effective.**'"*

And on refusing to make cost-efficiency match visual expectation:
> *"if I build six Battlecruisers, a lot of players look at that and imagine, 'there's nothing in the
> world that can defeat this…' And they're quite surprised when they run into 30 or 40 Hydralisks…
> **But we're not willing to make the Battlecruiser look wimpy just because if he encounters ten times
> his cost, he's going to lose.**"*

## 11. The one legibility critique — and it is not a developer

**Brandon Casteel**, strategy critic writing on Game Developer (*not* a developer):
> *"Warcraft 3 and to a greater extent Battle Realms have particularly dense counter relationships.
> I've played both games for years and **it's still frustrating to me that it's impossible in many
> cases to tell the counter relationship between unit types without just memorizing them.** … **Why
> one unit takes triple damage from a sword versus the unit next to it taking 25% damage from the same
> sword is a matter of memorizing tables and playing over and over — there's no good visual indicator
> for the player to use.**"*

⚠️ **No Blizzard developer has ever conceded the WC3 matrix was hard to learn.** Do not cite this as
first-party.
