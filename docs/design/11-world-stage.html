World stage — the component catalog

Every component the world map is made of, drawn in all its states, with the data field that drives it. This is an inventory before it is a design: the owner's question was “we have so many — I need to know what they are.” The architecture behind it is world-stage-ideal.md; this plate draws what that document decides.

It replaces plate 03’s world sections, which were authored 2026-08-22 — before the loam economy shipped. Grep plate 03 for loam, upkeep, warden or prospecting and it returns nothing; it also draws no end-turn control, no turn playback and no notification feed.

Three things every component here obeys. State is never carried by colour or opacity alone — 8–10% of male players cannot rely on red/green, and the shipped map currently encodes a sector’s health as opacity = 0.35 + 0.65 × stability/1000. Every number states its unit family, because four incompatible families are in play and a renderer that was not told which one it holds may not guess (GG-46). And every visible word is the player’s — engine tokens appear only in the annotation columns, which is where this plate deliberately shows them so the mapping stays auditable.

A · The sector node — the atom of the map

One card, and every state the engine can actually put it in. A sector carries four independent facts at once — who holds it, whether it can be kept, what is on it, and what it earns — so the node spends a different channel on each. Nothing below is invented: every state maps to a field on WorldSectorDto / WorldSlotDto, cited in the legend. Implements world-stage-ideal §4.1 and §4.9.

The rule this section exists to enforce (GG-27 / GG-30): state is never carried by colour or opacity alone. The shipped node encodes economic health as opacity = 0.35 + 0.65 × stability/1000 (SectorNode.tsx:49-52) and slot types as the bare letters S E M V L T ! $ (:29-39) — a continuous dim is unreadable as a value, and a letter is not a shape. Every node below states each fact on at least two of shape · border treatment · pattern · icon · text. 8–10% of male players cannot rely on red/green, and a dimmed card is indistinguishable from a card behind a scrim.

B · Lanes and the network

Lanes are the other half of the map: six kinds, each with its own rules about supply, direction and cost, plus five states a lane can be in. Every lane also carries two numbers the player is supposed to plan against — how far (Length) and how much crosses at once (Width) — so the drawing spends stroke weight on width and a printed figure on length. Why the lifeline is drawn on the map and not only in a panel. C · Fog — four states, and why this is the hard one

The server already answers this in four states, derived rather than stored (IntelLadder.StateOf, FactionIntel.cs:133-140): Unknown — never seen; Rumored — seen once, older than five turns; Scouted — remembered, within five turns; Watched — in sight right now. The client renders one of them well and the rest as a question mark. All four have to separate from each other and from a sector that is simply not yours.

The cost we are accepting, stated honestly. The owner decided (world-stage-ideal §8.2) to err toward distinctness: remembered ground must be unmistakably remembered. The doubled border, the parchment wash and the rotated stamp achieve that — and they are exactly the treatment Civ VI shipped, which produced a named complaint thread, "New Fog of War makes the map harder to use". Three specific costs, and what pays for each:
  • Border geometry is spent twice. The doubled edge (Scouted) and the ragged dash (Rumored) sit on the same border the node uses for ownership. Ownership keeps colour and crest; fog takes style and width. They must never both want a dash — which is why the unclaimed control case in C.1 is drawn beside the fog states rather than after them.
  • The parchment wash reduces contrast on remembered text. It is capped at 13% (Scouted) and 18% (Rumored) over the panel for that reason, and the wash sits under the content layer, never over it. Any lower and the state stops reading; any higher and the sector stops being plannable, which is the whole point of keeping the static facts.
  • A map of mostly-stale sectors becomes visually busy. Accepted deliberately: a map you have not scouted should feel like one. The mitigation is the lens — intel age is one of the map lenses, so a player who wants a clean read has one gesture to get it.
The spec owes a legibility check on the two stale states specifically — not merely a "can you tell them apart?" check, but "can you still plan a march against them?"

What this settles: four fog states, four treatments, each separating on border geometry, wash pattern, and an explicit text stamp — none on hue and none on opacity. The static/dynamic line is drawn as a table so it can be tested rather than argued, and the "not known" strip is explicit rather than an absence. Still open: whether Rumored should soften the numbers it shows (a remembered danger band of 4 might now be 5) — showing a stale figure as if it were exact is the one place this treatment could still lie, and the wire carries no confidence value to render.

D · Legions and forces — the marker in every state it can hold

A legion is the only thing on this map that moves, spends, starves and dies. One marker has to answer six questions at a glance — whose is it, where is it, what is it doing, can it still act, will it eat next week, and what is inside it — and a seventh for anything that is not yours: how sure am I? Every state below is drawn, named, and traced back to the field that produces it. world-stage-ideal §2 · §4.5

Colour is never the answer. Ownership is a shape (banner / diamond / circle) before it is a colour; supply is a sentence before it is a bar; stale intel is a hatch before it is a tint. 8–10% of male players cannot separate the red from the green, and the map's current encoding — opacity arithmetic — is unreadable for all of them.

Runway is an absolute turn number, not a countdown. The engine emits legion.runway:14 — turn fourteen, not fourteen turns. The marker must render both readings ("turn 14 — three turns out") because a player planning a march thinks in the countdown while a player reading the turn report thinks in the turn.

And two of these numbers cannot reach a client yet. Carried loam and the fighter/bearer role are real live state that no wire shape carries — the whole supply block above reaches a player today only as a raw narration string in the turn report. That is a field on a DTO, not a redesign. world-stage-ideal §2.2

Taking an order back is a real gesture, not a technicality. F.3 · Why a picker beats coupling lenses to zoom

Rejected — lens follows zoom depth

Elegant on paper: there is no picker to learn, no hotkey to remember, and the information you get is always the information the camera implies you want. Endless Space 2 ships this as its Scan view.

The failure is the one worth naming. When a layer's identity is defined only by how far you have zoomed, two layers converging is invisible. After an economy rework two of ES2's scans came to show the same thing, and players could not tell it was a bug — there was no name on screen to disagree with.

It also makes the zoom do two jobs at once, which is the same trap the zoom tiers are already being designed against: a tier may simplify, never trade one fact for another.

Taken — a named, exclusive picker

Every lens has a name on screen, a key, and a state you can point at. If two lenses ever start showing the same thing, a player can say which two — which makes it a bug report instead of a shrug.

The cost is a picker to learn, and auto-activation pays most of it back: the lenses a player would have had to remember are the ones that turn themselves on.

It also leaves zoom free to be only navigation, which is what it is good at, and what the no-minimap decision already depends on.

The lifelines flag becomes lens 4, not a checkbox. It already exists as an opt-in on the read endpoint because computing it costs something — so it is naturally a lens: requested when the player asks for it, not carried on every read. That is the pattern for any future lens with a server cost, and it is the reason the set is closed at six.

H.6 · The per-turn click budget

M · Numbers, units, and where a number came from

A renderer that was not told its unit family may not guess one. GG-46, Tier-1 Four families are in play on this stage and they are not interchangeable. Each card below shows the correct rendering above the wrong one, so the difference is a thing you can see rather than a rule you have to remember.

① Per-mille int0–1000 (and past it)

✓ Hold on the ground 24% StabilityMilli 240 → divide by 10, one decimal, trim a trailing zero
✗ Stability 240 the raw per-mille integer, printed at a player
✗ Stability 240% the same integer with a percent sign glued on — 10× wrong
✓ The Fracture runs at ×1.40 here FractureIntensityMilli 1400 — 1000 is the neutral point, and it goes above it. A renderer that clamps this to 100% hides the whole mechanic.
✗ Fracture 100% clamped, because someone assumed per-mille means "a fraction of one"
✓ ¾ of a march left MovementRemaining 750 — the name says nothing about its unit at all, and this is the field most likely to be rendered as a count
✗ 750 movement reads as 750 of something. It is 750‰ of one turn's march budget.
✓ 45% of the way down the Ridge road LaneProgressMilli 450
✓ Rough road — 8% worse going HazardMilli 80
✓ Pressure 12% PressureMilli 120 — real live state, written every turn, never projected onto a DTO
✗ Upkeep 0% a small non-zero per-mille rounded to nothing. Round away from zero at the display boundary — a real penalty never vanishes.

② Whole loam units longevery Loam* and Component*

✗ In store 0.012 loam divided by 1000 by a renderer that saw an integer and assumed per-mille

⚠ The trap: four fields named …Milli that hold whole loam units

StructureDef.CostMilli, and the three policy values behind it — LoamPolicy.WellCostMilli, WaystationCostMilli, GranaryCostMilli. They are compared directly against CarriedLoam and subtracted from it, so they are whole loam. Any renderer that trusts the name is wrong by 1000×. This is in the model, not the UI — which is exactly the ambiguity GG-46 exists to catch.

What the shipped values are

✓ A Well costs 200 loam wellCostMilli: 200 — two hundred loam, out of a legion's 180 carried, so the build is refused
✓ A Waystation costs 300 loam waystationCostMilli: 300
✓ A Granary costs 150 loam and keeps 300 more granaryCostMilli: 150, granaryCapacityBonus: 300 — the bonus has no Milli in its name and is the same unit as the cost

What a name-trusting renderer prints

✗ A Well costs 0.2 loam and the player cannot understand why a legion carrying 180 is refused
✗ A Well costs 20% the per-mille formatter, applied because the suffix said so
✗ Build — free loam-source-placeholder has CostMilli = 0, which is genuinely free; a renderer cannot tell that apart from a rounding artefact
The fix is not a special case in the renderer. The magnitude renderer requires an explicit unit family and refuses without one, and these four fields are declared loam at the projection boundary. Renaming them in the model is the real repair; declaring the family is what stops the bug reaching a screen in the meantime.

Three levels is the floor of the ledger. A fourth would be the tuning file, and the answer to "why is a garrison 2 loam a head" is a balance question, not a provenance one.
The arithmetic is the design. Every row is an operand, and reading down the column reproduces the number exactly: 10 + 8 + 10 + 9 = 37, then one combined step — 37 × 1400 × 1150 ÷ 1 000 000 = 59 — and 50 − 59 = −9.

The single division is the point, and an earlier draft got it wrong. It rounded twice (×1.40 → 52, then ×1.15 → 60) and printed 60 where the engine computes 59 — off by one, by construction, in the section whose whole subject is not lying about numbers. LoamUpkeep.For multiplies both per-mille factors and divides by 1 000 000 once, which is this repo's own rule: widen before multiplying, divide by 1000 last, exactly once. A ledger that rounds per row cannot reproduce it. A ledger whose rows do not add up to its total is worse than no ledger, because the player will trust it. Rounding happens once, at the display boundary, away from zero — the same direction the engine rounds.

And every operand is a real one — corrected 2026-09-03. The five rows are exactly the five arguments of LoamUpkeep.For(garrisonMembers, developmentLevel, dangerBand, intensityMilli, handicapMilli), in that order. An earlier draft drew a sixth row, “this month is heavier ×1.15”, which has no field behind it: there is no calendar term in that signature, and the calendar’s economic half is deferred to another module. Drawing it broke this very section’s rule in the section whose subject is not lying about numbers. It is now the faction upkeep handicap, which is real, is a declared balance lever, and which the engine already narrates as loam.handicap:1150.

WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.13 — the three obligations, and how each is met

DismissibleEsc closes the ledger without moving the pointer, and without closing the inspector under it. The ✕ does the same for pointer users.
HoverableThe pointer can travel from the number onto the ledger and through it without it vanishing — which is the whole point, since levels 2 and 3 are inside it.
PersistentIt stays until dismissed, the pointer leaves both the trigger and the ledger, or the underlying value changes. It never times out.
Lock gestureShift by default; dwell-time and middle-click are the alternatives, and which one is a player setting, not a constant.
KeyboardFocus the number and press Enter: the ledger opens locked, and its expandable rows are in the tab order. A hover-only ledger answers the question for half the players.
