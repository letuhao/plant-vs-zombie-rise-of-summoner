Run stages — Lawn & Battle

The two stages where the player is under time pressure. Both are places you go to act, so both are stages; neither is ever a panel. This is where GG-60 bites — legibility beats fiction on everything here — and where GG-15's split between acknowledging input and painting authority stops being theoretical.

Equipping mid-run takes effect on the next deploy, not on the creature already on the board. The panel says so rather than silently doing nothing.
The pause badge is in the HUD, not in the panel. "Did the wave keep running while I read this?" must never be a question — so the answer lives on the stage's own chrome, visible past the scrim. Range is shown, not described. The board paints reachable cells and target cells before the player commits, because an action with a min/max range and a line-of-sight requirement cannot be understood from a number.
Every action slot states its cost, and an unaffordable one says so by shape and colour both — dashed border, dimmed, red cost. Not colour alone. GG-27

Never disable without saying why. Every unaffordable and cooling state carries its reason. A greyed button with no explanation is indistinguishable from a broken one, and it is the most common dead end in game interfaces. GG-55

E · Truth under latency

Outcomes here are server-authoritative, and the client is forbidden from predicting them. So responsiveness is bought with acknowledgement, and the gap it opens is designed rather than hidden. GG-15 · GG-54

3 · Disconnected — the stage holds, it does not lie

⚠

Lost the thread

The battle is paused where it stands. Nothing was lost — it resumes when the connection returns.

The animation is the latency budget. A strike that takes 400 ms to play is 400 ms the round trip can use for free — which is how a game feels instant without predicting anything. That is the honest alternative to client-side prediction, which is banned here for good reasons. GG-34
